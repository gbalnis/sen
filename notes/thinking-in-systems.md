# Thinking in Systems

## Quick Summary

Donella Meadows’ *Thinking in Systems* is a practical primer on how complex systems behave and how to intervene in them responsibly. It introduces:

- Stocks and flows
- Feedback loops (reinforcing and balancing)
- Delays
- Resilience and self-organization
- Nonlinearity
- System traps
- Leverage points

## Key Ideas

### Stocks and Flows
- **Stock:** Accumulated value (open bugs, code quality, reliability, trust).
- **Flow:** Activities that change stocks (bug fixing, deployments, reporting).

### Feedback Loops
- **Reinforcing:** More begets more.
- **Balancing:** Pushes toward stability.

### Delays
- Delays between cause and effect can produce oscillation and overshoot.

### Resilience and Self-Organization
- Healthy systems absorb shocks and adapt.
- Self-organizing systems can evolve new restorative mechanisms.

### System Traps
- Policy resistance
- Tragedy of the commons
- Drift to low performance
- Escalation
- Success to the successful
- Shifting the burden
- Rule beating
- Seeking the wrong goal

### Leverage Points
From lowest to highest leverage:
1. Parameters
2. Buffers
3. Stock-flow structure
4. Delays
5. Balancing loops
6. Reinforcing loops
7. Information flows
8. Rules
9. Self-organization
10. Goals
11. Paradigms
12. Transcending paradigms

## Metrics as Sensors

### SPACE
- Satisfaction
- Performance
- Activity
- Collaboration
- Efficiency/Flow

### DORA
- Deployment Frequency
- Lead Time for Changes
- Change Failure Rate
- Mean Time To Restore

## Playbook

### Diagnose
- Map stocks
- Identify flows
- Locate loops
- Find delays
- Assess sensors
- Spot traps

### Intervene
- Adjust parameters where helpful
- Improve information flows
- Align incentives and goals
- Enable self-organization
- Challenge paradigms

### Measure
- Define expected outcomes
- Watch side effects
- Iterate quickly

## Final Take

As an Engineering Manager, the key is shaping system structure: feedback loops, delays, and flows that enable teams to do their best work.
